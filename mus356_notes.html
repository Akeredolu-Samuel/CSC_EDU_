Music Fundamentals

The Staff

A staff (or stave) consists of 5 lines and 4 spaces used for writing musical notes and signs.

The Clef

A musical sign placed at the beginning of the staff to give a permanent name and pitch to the lines and spaces.

  • Treble / G Clef: Used for high-pitched singers or instruments.
  • Bass / F Clef: Used for notating music for lower pitch singers or instruments.

Treble Clef Lines & Spaces

Lines (Bottom to Top):
E (Every) - G (Good) - B (Boy) - D (Deserves) - F (Food)

Spaces (Bottom to Top):
F - A - C - E (Face)

Bass Clef Lines & Spaces

Lines (Bottom to Top):
G (Good) - B (Boys) - D (Deserve) - F (Food) - A (Always)

Spaces (Bottom to Top):
A (All) - C (Cows) - E (Eat) - G (Grass)

Solfa Notes & Technical Names

  • Doh: Tonic
  • Re: Supertonic
  • Mi: Mediant
  • Fah: Subdominant
  • Soh: Dominant
  • Lah: Submediant
  • Ti: Leading Note
  • Doh¹: Octave
Genres of Music
  • Classical or Western Chorus: Serious music with rules and western text (e.g., Hallelujah Chorus by J.G Handel).
  • African Chorus: Classical music in an African context with African lyrics.
  • R&B (Rhythm and Blues): Coolness and calmness in vocal performance (e.g., R.Kelly, Whitney Houston).
  • Jazz & Blues: Originated from black America, mostly instrumental (e.g., Charlie Parker, Kenny G).
  • Highlife & Afro: Strong rhythmic melody and heavy horns line (e.g., Fela, Victor Uwaifo).
  • Reggae: Originated from Jamaica, known for strong rhythmic baseline (e.g., Bob Marley, Majek Fashek).
  • Hip-Hop: Strong rhythmic pattern of beats originated by black americans.
  • Juju: Originated from highlife, distinguished by string rhythmic pattern (e.g., King Sunny Ade).
  • Fuji & Apala: Originated from Islamic believers in Nigeria, pure percussion (e.g., Sikiru Ayinde Barrister).
  • Traditional Music: Expresses the way of life and do's/don'ts of a society.
Stage Performance

Live presentation of music by musicians on a stage in front of an audience, encompassing musical expression, technical proficiency, and audience engagement.

Types of Performance Stages

  • Proscenium Stage: Traditional stage with a framed opening (proscenium arch), providing clear separation between performers and audience.
  • Thrust Stage: Extends into the audience area on three sides for a more intimate experience.
  • Arena Stage: Theater-in-the-round, surrounded by audience seating on all sides.
  • Outdoor Stage: Open-air venues like parks or amphitheaters.
  • Platform Stage: Simple, raised platform without surrounding infrastructure.

Performance Tips

Practice: Rehearse music and stage movements thoroughly.
Confidence: Project enthusiasm and passion.
Stage Presence: Make eye contact, use body language.
Connect: Acknowledge and interact with the audience.
Music Licensing in Nigeria

The transfer of exclusive or non-exclusive rights permitting reproduction, publishing, performance, or distribution of musical works for commercial purposes.

Types of Music Licensing

  • Master License and Mechanical License: Granted by the owner of the master recording for commercial exploitation.
  • Synchronization License: Grants the right to synchronize music with visual media (movies, games, TV).
  • Broadcast or Performance License: Grants the right to perform, stream, or play sound recordings in public (radio, TV, concerts). Administered by CMOs like COSON and MCSN.
  • Print License: Grants rights to transcribe or print physical copies of sheet music.

Copyright Organizations

  • COSON (Copyright Society of Nigeria): Sole CMO approved by the Nigerian Copyright Commission.
  • MCSN (Music Copyright Society of Nigeria): Incorporated collective association handling rights previously managed by UK's PRS and MCPS.
Western Orchestra Instruments

Classification

  • String Instruments: Sound produced by vibrating strings (plucking, bowing). Examples: Violin, Viola, Cello, Double Bass, Guitar, Harp.
  • Woodwind Instruments: Sound produced by vibrating air within a tube across a mouthpiece or reed. Examples: Flute, Clarinet, Saxophone, Bassoon, Piccolo.
  • Brass Instruments: Sound produced by vibrating lips against a cup-shaped mouthpiece resonating in a metal tube. Examples: Trumpet, Trombone, French Horn, Euphonium, Tuba.
  • Percussion Instruments: Sound produced by striking, shaking, or scraping.
    • Definite Pitch: Can play a tune (Xylophone, Marimba, Harp, Timpani/Kettle Drum, Tubular Bells).
    • Indefinite Pitch: Produce rhythms only (Cymbals, Triangle, Conga, Snare Drum, Bass Drum, Maracas).

The Piano (Bonus)

Invented around 1720 by Bartholomeo Cristofori (Italian). Originally called Pianoforte. Types include Upright, Baby Grand, and Grand Piano.
