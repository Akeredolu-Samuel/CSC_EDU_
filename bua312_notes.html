Introduction & Definitions
Chapter 1 — BUA 312

Several regulatory bodies in Nigeria and internationally have defined 'small business' from different perspectives. Below are the key definitions you must know.

SMEDAN Definition

Definition

A small business has 10–49 employees with an asset base (excluding land and building) valued between ₦5 million and ₦50 million.

CategoryEmployeesCapital Outlay
MicroBelow 10Below ₦500,000
Small10–49₦5m – less than ₦50m
Medium50–199₦50m – less than ₦500m

Other Nigerian Definitions

NTA
Nigeria Tax Act
Any business earning up to ₦50m per annum with fixed assets not exceeding ₦250m. Professional service businesses are excluded.
CAMA
Companies & Allied Matters Act 2020
Annual turnover ≤ ₦120m and net assets ≤ ₦60m. No foreign member. Director holds ≥ 51% of share capital.
CBN
Central Bank of Nigeria
Assets between ₦5m and ₦500m (excl. land & building); 11–200 employees.
BOI
Bank of Industry (2020)
Small: 11–50 employees; assets ₦5m–₦100m; turnover ≤ ₦100m; loan ₦1m–₦100m.

European Union (EU) Definition

EU Definition

Fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding €10 million. The entity must be independent.

Characteristics & Types

Characteristics of Small Business

01
Few Employees
Small workforce relative to larger enterprises.
02
Low Investment
Low capital outlay and annual turnover.
03
Owner = Manager
Managers are often the owners themselves.
04
Simple Structure
Flat, simple organizational hierarchy.
05
Flexible
Able to adapt quickly to market changes.
06
Customer Proximity
Maintain close contact with customers and clients.
07
Fast Decisions
Decision making is often quick due to flat hierarchy.
08
Personalized Style
Personalized management style by the owner.

Types of Small Business Enterprises

1
Servicing
Offers intangible goods (services). Examples: salons, repair shops, consultancy, event planning.
2
Retailing
Sells products directly to end users. Examples: clothing stores, grocery shops, online stores.
3
Wholesaling
Sells products in bulk to retailers or businesses. Examples: distributors, bulk food sellers.
4
Manufacturing
Converts raw materials into finished/semi-finished goods. Examples: bakeries, garment factories.
5
Agriculture
Cultivates plants/animals for food or raw materials. Examples: poultry farms, fish farming.
6
Extractive Business
Extracts natural resources. Examples: mining, logging, fishing.
3
Advantages & Disadvantages
Advantages
  • Low capital required for establishment
  • Provide employment opportunities
  • Feeder to large enterprises
  • Help conserve foreign exchange
  • Promote indigenous technology
  • Reduce rural-urban migration
  • Encourage capital formation
  • Quick decision making
  • Flexible operations
  • Not subject to rigid legal control
Disadvantages
  • High rate of business failure
  • Owners may lose private assets to creditors
  • Often lack sufficient capital
  • Excessive control by owner
  • Death of owner may end the business
  • Weak ability to face competition
  • Lack of delegation of authority
  • No separation between business and owner
  • Limited skills and competencies
  • No planned staff training

Socio-Economic Contributions

Small businesses play a major role in the economic and social development of any nation.

  • Transformation of traditional indigenous industry into modern enterprise
  • Stimulation of indigenous entrepreneurship and technology
  • Creation of jobs and employment in the community
  • Provision of social welfare services
  • Redistribution of income and wealth
  • Dispersal and diversification of economic activities
  • Utilization of local raw materials and human resources
  • Translating new information into new market techniques and goods
  • Seeking and discovering economic opportunities
  • Evaluating economic opportunities

Importance of Entrepreneurship in National Development

  • Improves overall social, political, economic and technological well-being of a country
  • Increases productivity, standard of living, and enables poverty reduction
  • Creates employment and drives sustainable growth
  • Increases investment and savings
  • Drives innovation and technological advancement
  • Develops social resources and provides an enabling business environment

Problems & Symptoms of Failure

Small businesses have limitations that often account for failure. It is important to distinguish between symptoms (warning signs) and the actual problems.

5 Key Symptoms of Business Failure
  • Deteriorating working capital
  • High debt ratio
  • Declining sales
  • Declining profit
  • Declining capacity utilisation

Starting and Launching a Small Business

To effectively plan and launch a small business, certain processes must be followed:

1
Triggering Event
Factors that give birth to a new venture — psychological, economical, sociological, or historical in nature.
2
Self-Appraisal
Asking yourself if you can start and sustain the business. Involves diligence, initiative, risk tolerance, technical competence, and leadership.
3
Conceptualization of Business Idea
Formulate the core business concept clearly.
4
Analysing the Opportunity
Evaluate feasibility and market potential.
5
Developing a Business Plan
Create a credible, detailed business plan.
6
Assembling Resources
Gather financial, human, and material resources needed.
7
Location Decision
Identify site, location, and layout for the business.
8
Legal Form
Choose a legal form of business organisation (sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability, etc.).
9
Launching the Business
Begin full operations.
10
Administrative Support System
Establish policies, communication systems, record-keeping, budgeting, and reward systems.

Entrepreneurial Skills
Definition

Entrepreneurial skills are the ability, knowledge, ideas, and competence required to survive in any business setting.

Types of Entrepreneurial Skills

01
Managerial Skills
Planning, organising, controlling, leading, and directing.
02
Personal Skills
Skills in production, machine operation, etc.
03
Technical Skills
Technical know-how to run business operations.
04
Interpersonal Skills
Interaction, communication, and negotiation.
05
Financial Skills
Managing finances, budgeting, and accounting.

Process of Developing Entrepreneurial Skills

  • Self development — self determination and commitment
  • Conversation with people (networking)
  • Mentorship and nurturing
  • Practical experience
  • Education and training

Challenges in Developing Entrepreneurial Skills

  • Lack of access to quality education
  • Limited financial resources
  • Fear of failure
  • Inadequate mentorship
  • Poor business environment

Ability to identify and evaluate opportunities.

Financing Small Businesses

Access to finance is one of the most critical factors in the survival and growth of small businesses. Funds come from internal or external sources.

Internal Sources
  • Personal savings of the owner
  • Retained earnings / profits ploughed back
  • Sale of personal assets
External Sources
  • Commercial bank loans
  • Microfinance banks
  • Cooperative societies and thrift
  • Government grants (CBN, BOI)
  • Venture capital and angel investors
  • Trade credit from suppliers
  • Hire purchase and leasing
  • NGO and development agency funding

Challenges of Financing in Nigeria

  • Lack of collateral for bank loans
  • High interest rates
  • Poor credit history
  • Inadequate financial records
  • Limited awareness of available funding programmes
  • Bureaucratic bottlenecks in accessing government funds
10
Location, Marketing & Labour

Business Location Decision Factors

  • Proximity to target customers and markets
  • Availability of raw materials
  • Availability of labour
  • Infrastructure (roads, power, water)
  • Cost of premises (rent or purchase)
  • Proximity to competitors
  • Government regulations and zoning laws
  • Safety and security of the area

Marketing for Small Businesses

Key Concept

Marketing involves identifying customer needs and satisfying them profitably. The core framework is the 4Ps: Product, Price, Place, and Promotion.

Common tools: word of mouth, social media (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp Business), local advertising, promotions, and customer relationship management.

Labour Management Issues

  • Recruitment and selection of employees
  • Staff training and development
  • Motivation and incentives
  • Compliance with labour laws (minimum wage, work hours)
  • Managing family employees who may complicate decisions

Accounting for Small Businesses

  • Recording income and expenses (bookkeeping)
  • Preparing financial statements — income statement, balance sheet, cash flow
  • Managing cash flow to avoid insolvency
  • Filing tax returns and meeting regulatory obligations
  • Using accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks, Sage)

Small Business Environment
Definition

The internal and external factors that affect the operation, survival, and growth of small businesses.

Internal Environment (Controllable)

  • Management structure and style
  • Financial resources and capital base
  • Human resources (skills, experience)
  • Technology and equipment
  • Organizational culture

External — Micro Environment

  • Customers and their preferences
  • Competitors and their strategies
  • Suppliers and their reliability
  • Creditors and financial institutions

External — Macro Environment (PESTLE)

P
Political / Legal
Government policies, regulations, tax laws.
E
Economic
Inflation, interest rates, exchange rates.
S
Socio-Cultural
Demographics, consumer trends, cultural values.
T
Technological
Innovations, automation, e-commerce.
E
Environmental
Climate, natural resources.

Challenges in Nigeria & Prospects

Major Challenges

1
Access to Finance
Difficulty obtaining loans due to lack of collateral, high interest rates, and poor credit history.
2
Infrastructure Deficiency
Poor roads, unstable electricity, and inadequate water raise operating costs significantly.
3
Multiple Taxation
Various taxes and levies from multiple government tiers reduce profitability.
4
Lack of Managerial Skills
Many owners lack formal training in management, marketing, and finance.
5
Competition
Unfair competition from large corporations and imported goods.
6
Insecurity
Theft, vandalism, and general insecurity increase risks and costs.
7
Poor Record Keeping
Absence of proper accounts makes it difficult to access credit or attract investors.
8
Low Patronage
Low consumer purchasing power limits market size.
9
Lack of Technology
Limited access to modern technology hampers productivity and efficiency.
10
Unfavourable Policies
Regulatory inconsistency and bureaucracy create an unfavourable environment.
